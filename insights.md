## 💡 ANALYSIS FINDINGS & INSIGHTS 
### 📈 SALES PERFORMANCE 
#### Overall results  
Overall sales performance is strong but uneven across months and product categories. 
Between 1 January 2025 and 15 May 2026, the business sold 2,467 items, generating 
$294,348 in revenue and $99,064 in profit, for an overall profit margin of about 34%, even 
though across all products sold about 32% of sales were made at a loss. Clothing was the best-performing category, contributing 46% of total revenue and 47% of total profit. However, this result may partly reflect its broader product assortment, as Clothing contains three products. 
#### Monthly trends and seasonality 
Monthly trends show possible seasonal patterns and performance spikes. In 2025, May recorded the 
highest revenue at $21,678 with a profit of $7,792, while October delivered the highest 
profit at $10,800 despite slightly lower revenue of $20,405. In 2026, January posted the 
highest revenue at $20,090 and the highest profit at $9,268, making it the strongest single 
month of the year. 
#### Monthly profit margins 
Profitability at the monthly level was uneven. Profit margins ranged from a very strong 53% 
in October 2025 and May 2026 to weaker but still positive margins of 14% and 13% in 
December 2025 and January 2025, respectively. This indicates that profitability varied 
significantly across months, likely driven by shifts in product mix, pricing, discount intensity, 
or underlying cost structure. 
### 👥 CUSTOMER BEHAVIOUR 
#### Purchase patterns and loyalty 
Customer behaviour currently leans heavily toward one-time purchases rather than 
long-term loyalty. Most customers are one-time buyers (more than 60%), with only a small 
share in repeat (2–3 purchases) or loyal segments (4+ purchases). This suggests the business 
is effective at acquiring new customers but less successful at retaining them and growing 
customer lifetime value. 
#### Channel usage and retention opportunities 
Channel usage is relatively balanced but slightly skewed toward digital. Of all items sold, 51% 
were purchased online and 49% in-store, indicating that customers actively use both 
channels.  
### 💰 PRODUCT PROFITABILITY 
#### Category-level profitability 
Product profitability is concentrated in a few key items and does not always align with 
volume. Clothing is the most profitable category, driving nearly half of total revenue and 
total profit (46% and 47%, respectively), while Accessories follow with 23% of total revenue 
and 22% of total profit. Electronics and Home categories contribute less than 16% to both 
revenue and profit, indicating a weaker role in overall performance. 
#### Product-level insights 
At the product level, volume and profit are not perfectly aligned. Sneakers are the top 
product by quantity (410 units) and generate $48,250 in revenue (16%) and $14,854 in profit 
(15%). T-shirts, however, deliver the highest profit of $17,605 (18%) from slightly lower 
revenue of $44,335 (15%). Headphones (383 units) and Jeans (380 units), the second- and 
third-highest products by quantity, produced average revenues and profits of $45,111 and 
$14,841. This shows that the best-selling product is not necessarily the most profitable, and 
that margin structure is as important as volume. 
#### Discount impact on product margins 
The discount analysis shows that profitability does not improve consistently as discounts 
increase. At a 10% discount level, the average profit margin is positive (+5%), whereas at 0% 
and 5% discounts, the margins remain negative (-11% and -8%). At higher discount levels 
(15% and 20%), profit margins deteriorate sharply to -43% and -17%, indicating that deeper 
discounts significantly erode profitability and are not compensated by higher sales. 
### 🌍 REGIONAL AND CHANNEL PERFORMANCE 
#### Regional efficiency 
Profit conversion across regions ranges from roughly $19,000 to $27,000. While 
the North generates the highest revenue at $81,211, the South achieves comparable profit 
from a lower revenue base of $71,008. This indicates that the South is more successful at 
turning sales into profit and may benefit from a more favourable product mix, pricing 
strategy, or cost structure. 
#### Channel profitability 
By sales channel, in-store is more profitable despite lower volume. In-store sales generated 
$50,952 in profit from $144,694 in revenue and 1,194 items sold, while online sales 
produced $48,112 in profit from a higher revenue base of $149,654 and 1,273 items sold. 
This suggests that in-store transactions have stronger margins and that online sales may 
involve higher discounting, fees, or costs that compress profitability despite higher revenue 
and volume.
